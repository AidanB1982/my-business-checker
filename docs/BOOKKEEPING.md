# Plain-English bookkeeping — Advanced

`/bookkeeping` is a first native bookkeeping release for sole traders and individual landlords. Creating books, posting entries, reversals, locking periods and signing off reconciliations require an active Advanced subscription **in the database**, including direct RPC callers. Account records are private to their owner. Expired subscribers retain read/export access to their existing books. Public visitors see a feature description; no production sample records are created.

## Enable it

Run **only** `supabase/migrations/202611030001_bookkeeping.sql` once in the existing Supabase SQL Editor, after the existing migrations. It is transactional. It creates five tables and the controlled functions/policies. No Edge Function, service key or new environment variable is needed. Deploying Next.js does not apply SQL automatically. Until migration, the app returns `BOOKS_SETUP_REQUIRED`.

## Start a book

Choose a sole-trader business or one landlord property, its name and its first date. Each property is a separate book; consolidated portfolio/business reports are not included. One bank account and cash-in-hand account are supported per book. Use GBP gross amounts, with accrual bookkeeping: invoices recognise income when recorded; bills recognise costs when recorded. This is not a cash-basis tax-return engine and has no VAT control/returns. VAT-registered businesses need an accountant-reviewed VAT workflow outside this release.

Confirm balances immediately before the start date. The system balances net assets/liabilities to opening owner capital. Starting balances are fixed once confirmed. All-zero balances require explicit confirmation. Opening receivable/payable amounts are control totals; use the opening-receipt / opening-bill-payment actions to clear them without recognising income/expense again. They are not individual aged opening invoices. Choose dates and opening values carefully; there is no opening-balance editing or migration importer in this release.

## Guided records

- Current customer/rent receipts and costs paid now; record the business portion only.
- Unpaid invoice/rent and supplier-bill records with counterparty/reference. These are ledger records, not legal invoice generation or delivery. Select the original when recording a partial or full payment. Paying a bill or collecting an invoice does not recognise the expense/income twice. Outstanding invoices/bills show age since record date, not contractual overdue dates. There are no reminders or credit-note workflows.
- Owner contributions, drawings, loans and mortgage funds actually received into the bank. Borrowing is a liability; drawings reduce equity. For direct-to-solicitor mortgage completions or complex acquisitions, obtain accountant help before entering records.
- Loan/mortgage payments split into principal and interest. Principal reduces the liability, interest enters bookkeeping costs. Repayments cannot exceed recorded principal as of the date.
- Equipment/property purchases from existing recorded funds, a manually calculated depreciation entry, tenant deposit receipts/refunds, and bank/cash transfers. Refundable tenant deposits are liabilities, not rental income. There is no deposit protection, depreciation-policy or tax allowance engine. Depreciation and deposit refunds cannot exceed their recorded asset/liability at the date.

Entries are reviewed locally before explicit posting. The API compiles the guided event into journal lines. Database functions atomically post header/lines and enforce paid access, ownership, dates, locks and balanced journals. Integer pence avoids floating-point journal amounts. Deferred database constraint triggers independently reject empty/unbalanced committed journals. Direct authenticated edits/deletes/inserts to all ledger tables are denied. Posting requests and reversals use unique request IDs for retries.

## Reports and controls

Profit and loss covers the chosen date range. Balance sheet and trial balance include all posted history through the chosen end date, including opening balances and accumulated earlier profits. Bank overdrafts are shown as liabilities. Opening balances do not create current income. Cash movement is net bank/cash movement per event; internal transfers are excluded. Negative reports remain visible. Click P&L account names or trial-balance accounts to inspect the ledger. Money-owed lists use balances as of the report end date. These are **bookkeeping reports, not statutory accounts or taxable-profit calculations**. Landlord finance-cost restrictions/relief, capital allowances, property valuations, owner income tax, VAT, HMRC/MTD submissions and Companies House filing are outside this release.

Correct a posted entry by a full reversal and then record the correct entry. The original is retained. Reverse invoice/bill payments first before reversing the original document. Opening balances and reversal entries cannot themselves be reversed. A correction dated in a later period affects that later period, preserving earlier reports. There is no arbitrary journal editor, partial credit-note wizard or editable posted record.

Reconcile manually against actual bank statements: use the opening bank balance for the first check and the previous signed-off closing balance thereafter. Tick bank entries, enter the closing balance and confirm only a zero difference. No entry can be matched twice, and selected entries must belong to this book and fall on/before the statement date. Unmatched older entries remain outstanding and can clear on a later statement. New bank entries cannot be backdated into a reconciled period. Statement sign-offs cannot be removed in this release. No bank feed, statement CSV import or automatic matching is included.

Period locks are forward-only. Once locked, no journal can be dated in that period. There is no unlock action. Owners must check reports and bank statements first. The latest 100 control events are shown; all posted journals remain in the exported ledger. Limits are 20 books per account and 5,000 journal entries per book.

CSV ledger and complete-book JSON exports remain available after expiry. JSON is not an import format. Print/save-PDF displays the current report dates; CSV/JSON include the whole book. No receipts/attachments, multi-user permissions, multi-currency, bank integrations, payroll or tax filing are claimed. These require subsequent separate work.

## Customer and supplier accounts

After the first bookkeeping migration, apply `supabase/migrations/202611030002_customer_supplier_accounts.sql` **once**. It adds named contact records, journal-account links and allocations of existing opening control balances. It does not rewrite posted journal headers or amounts. No existing free-text names are automatically combined into accounts. There is no additional credential or Edge Function.

Use Customer accounts (including tenants) and Supplier accounts to create separate named records with references, email, phone, address and notes. Each has an ID within its book; the same name in another book is a different account. Within a type/book, names must be distinct. If a person is both customer and supplier, create both accounts; their debts are not automatically netted.

New unpaid invoices and bills require the appropriate account. Direct paid-at-once income/purchases may optionally use an account, producing activity with no outstanding debt. Invoice/bill payments and reversals derive the account from the original document, preventing accidental allocation to a different party. Renaming a contact does not rewrite posted header names. Contact edits have version checks. Archived records retain their balances and history; new business is blocked but existing debts may still be settled.

Statements show chronological added/reduced amounts and a running balance. Customer positive balances are owed to you; supplier positive balances are owed by you. Screen statement rows use the report date range and bring earlier balances forward; the account list totals include all history through the report end date. Statement CSV exports include the whole history through that date and identify the account/book. No statement emails are sent. Negative balances/credits remain visible rather than silently offsetting another account.

Older records stay unassigned until linked. Assign the original invoice/bill or paid event in “Assign or correct transaction accounts”; its payments and reversals follow the same account automatically. Reassignment changes account metadata only, is audited, and does not post another financial transaction. The control check compares named account totals with the receivable/payable ledger, displaying the unassigned/unmatched difference explicitly.

Opening amounts can be allocated to named accounts only from the **existing original opening receivable/payable totals**. These allocations are not new invoices, income, expenses or journals. Their total cannot exceed the original control amount. Opening-balance receipts/payments require a named account with sufficient allocated original debt; allocations cannot be reduced below already assigned opening payments. To migrate older opening receipts, first allocate the original amount and then link the receipt. A temporarily negative unassigned difference signals that older reductions still need linking. Opening invoices are not individually aged or matched by invoice number in this version.

All account creation, edits, archive changes, allocations and assignments require Advanced at the API and database. Private contact records and statement exports remain readable by their owner after expiry. The fixed limit is 1,000 combined customer/supplier accounts per book. Contact JSON data is included in the full-book export. No arbitrary balance overrides, credit-note workflow, automated reminders or account merging is implemented.

## Itemised invoices without uploads

Apply `supabase/migrations/202611030003_invoices.sql` once **after** the bookkeeping and customer/supplier migrations. It creates structured invoice/profile/number-sequence tables only. There is no Storage bucket, upload endpoint, PDF binary storage, OneDrive integration or new credential.

The Invoices section lists native documents alongside earlier invoice/bill journal records. Earlier records are labelled ledger-only and are not posted again or given invented line items/due dates. New documents are saved as drafts first; drafts do not affect accounting balances. Draft edits use versions, and unposted drafts can be deleted. Record numbers, quantities (up to 3 decimal places), unit prices (2 decimal places), line descriptions and dates. Each line rounds to pennies before totals are summed. Up to 50 items are supported, with a positive GBP total up to £1 billion.

**Customer invoices:** save the issuer’s legal name, optional trading name, address, contact/payment details and explicit non-VAT-registration declaration. Add the customer’s billing address to their account. Review a draft, then issue it. Issue assigns a unique `INV-` number from one sequence per owner across all their books and atomically posts income/receivables through the existing ledger. It skips existing manual customer references. VAT-registered customer invoices, discounts, credit notes, advance deposits and asset purchases are not supported by this first invoice workflow. The supply date must be within the book and no later than the invoice date; future-dated drafts cannot be posted early. The invoice date is the ledger posting date. Supply date is preserved on the document; period-end accrual/cut-off adjustments are not automatic and need a separate review.

**Supplier invoices:** enter the supplier’s own invoice number, gross line amounts and expense categories. Recording posts the categorised costs/payable once. Active duplicate numbers for the same supplier are rejected. The supplier’s original file remains outside the app. Any printable supplier page is clearly an **internal record**, not an invoice issued on the supplier’s behalf or a replacement VAT invoice. Supplier records may be entered without setting up customer invoice business details.

Issuing/recording is atomic and idempotent. Posted documents cannot be edited or deleted. Issuer and recipient details are preserved at the point of posting, so later profile/contact edits do not rewrite issued documents. An issued itemised document also cannot silently be reassigned to a different account; use a ledger reversal and a corrected document. Existing invoice payment methods update the balances; the register shows draft, unpaid, part-paid, overdue, paid or reversed status. Due dates are captured for new documents, while old records retain an explicit “not captured” date. This is not automated payment collection or emailing.

Open a posted document’s print/PDF view and use the browser’s **Print / save as PDF** to save it locally. Customer invoices show the frozen details and non-VAT wording. Supplier printouts show the internal-record notice. Only the signed-in book owner can access that view; sending its app URL to a customer does not grant access. Download a PDF yourself if you want to send it outside the app. Current recorded outstanding balance may change with payments, while the original invoice fields remain frozen.

Full itemised data and preserved party details are available through **Download full invoice data** on each document. The existing whole-book JSON includes invoice summaries, not every line/snapshot. Keep individual invoice exports/PDFs as needed. Existing records/print views remain readable after Advanced expires; creating, editing, deleting drafts, changing business details and posting require active Advanced. No invoice files are uploaded, and PDFs are generated on demand rather than stored.

## Account and invoice validation

Additional engine and PostgreSQL tests cover running balances, partial settlements, archived/renamed accounts, separate customer/supplier signs, cross-book access, opening allocations, duplicate retries, inherited payments, reassignment without changing financial lines and prevention of direct posting-core bypass. Browser fixtures exercise account creation, invoice/bill allocation, partial payments, statement CSV and mobile layout. These are test-only fixtures, not production sample records.

Engine tests check double entry, profit/cash differences, owner movements, borrowing, mortgage principal/interest, deposits, unpaid documents, partial payments, prior-period equity, overdrafts, transfers, reversals, dates, penny validation and safe CSV. PostgreSQL/PGlite tests execute the migration and verify subscription enforcement, isolation, direct-write denial, balance constraints, duplicate retries, locks, reconciliation and overpayments. API tests verify the server builds trusted lines and rejects forged fields. Browser tests bundle the real UI with a local test-only API fixture; no auth bypass is shipped. Production multi-account sign-in, real statement checks and accountant review still require the deployed migration and actual users.
